When your mind won't let you settle

You replay conversations, wonder whether someone is upset with you, or feel responsible for anticipating everything that could go wrong. Even when you have time to rest, your mind keeps working.

Maybe you're getting through the day and meeting your responsibilities, but it takes more energy than anyone realizes. Or anxiety has started making your world smaller: you avoid decisions, difficult conversations, or experiences you want to have because the uncertainty feels overwhelming.

You may understand logically that something is probably okay and still struggle to feel that way.

Anxiety has a purpose. It can also become exhausting.

Anxiety is part of being human. It helps us prepare, notice potential problems, and take care of things that matter. We don't need to eliminate every anxious feeling to live well.

But when worry becomes constant, reassurance never seems to last, or you feel unable to move forward without knowing exactly how things will turn out, anxiety can begin to take over.

In therapy, we'll get curious about what your anxiety is responding to, what it may be trying to protect you from, and how your ways of coping might bring temporary relief while keeping you stuck.

We'll also make room for the realities of your life. Sometimes you're carrying too much, facing a difficult situation, or responding to expectations that leave little room to be human.

How we can work together

Our work may include:

  • Recognizing patterns of overthinking, self-doubt, avoidance, and reassurance-seeking.
  • Practicing grounding and mindfulness to reconnect with the present.
  • Learning to respond to anxious thoughts without having to resolve every one.
  • Building your ability to tolerate uncertainty and uncomfortable emotions.
  • Taking meaningful steps toward what you want, even when anxiety comes along.

I bring warmth, curiosity, and practical tools to this work. We'll explore both what happens in the moment and the experiences and expectations that have shaped how you respond.

The goal is for you to have more choice. Anxiety can ride in the passenger seat without deciding where your life goes.
