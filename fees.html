Therapy that centers you

Paying privately for therapy can offer several benefits:

  • Your care is guided by your needs. Rather than structuring our work around insurance requirements, we can collaborate on goals, explore what feels most important to you, and determine the focus and pace of therapy based on your individual circumstances and clinical needs.
  • A more personal approach to care. I value being responsive, intentional, and fully present in my work with clients. Maintaining a practice that reflects these priorities allows me to be thoughtful about my caseload and the quality of care I provide.
  • More privacy from insurance processes. When you choose not to submit claims to your health insurance, your therapy sessions and associated diagnoses are not shared with your insurer through the claims process. Confidentiality still has legal and professional limits, and other records or disclosures may be subject to applicable laws.

Can I use my insurance if you're out of network?

Although I do not bill many insurances directly, your health insurance plan may offer out-of-network (OON) benefits that reimburse you for a portion of your therapy costs.

Here's how to find out:

Step 1

Call the member services number on your insurance card.

Ask whether your plan includes out-of-network benefits for outpatient mental health or psychotherapy services.

Step 2

Ask these questions:

  • Do I have out-of-network benefits for outpatient mental health therapy?
  • What is my out-of-network deductible, and how much have I met so far?
  • After meeting my deductible, what percentage of the allowed amount does my plan reimburse?
  • Is there a separate out-of-network deductible or coinsurance for mental health services?
  • Is there a limit on the number of therapy sessions covered per year?
  • Do I need a referral or prior authorization?
  • What documentation do you require to submit an out-of-network claim?
  • Can I submit claims myself, and how do I do so?
Step 3

Ask about reimbursement.

Your insurance company can explain how it calculates reimbursement and what portion of the cost you may be responsible for. Reimbursement is based on your specific plan's terms and may be less than the amount you pay for a session.

Step 4

Request a superbill.

A superbill is a detailed receipt containing information your insurer may require to process an out-of-network claim. I can provide a superbill for you to submit to your insurance company, if applicable.

A helpful tip: Ask your insurance company to explain your benefits before beginning therapy. Benefits and reimbursement vary by plan, and verification does not guarantee payment.

Good Faith Estimate. Under the No Surprises Act, you have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges if you are not using insurance. Learn more at cms.gov/nosurprises.
